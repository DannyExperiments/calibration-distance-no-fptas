# Q460 literature and original target

Search completed 27 November 2026. High confidence in the match to the original definition and the distinctions below. Moderate confidence in the bounded search for another hardness result for the same approximation problem. The report makes no priority claim.

## Exact target

The pinned catalog asks for additive \(\epsilon\)-approximation in time polynomial in the explicit rational input bit length and \(1/\epsilon\). For a distribution on a finite feature alphabet and binary labels, the objective is the expected absolute change from the supplied deterministic forecast \(f\) to the closest deterministic predictor \(g\) exactly calibrated under that same distribution. This is an explicit-distribution optimization problem. An atom receives a single output and retains its whole mass. Masses, label means and forecasts together specify the input. The runtime is polynomial in the numerical parameter \(1/\epsilon\), unlike the degree in a PTAS. [Pinned Q460](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/computer_science_ai/classical-statistical-machine-learning.md#q460).

## Direct predecessor

Mingda Qiao, *Computational and Statistical Hardness of Calibration Distance*, arXiv:2603.18391v2, revised 14 October 2026. Section 1.2 defines the objective. Section 2, Fact 1 and Definition 1 identify it with minimizing the cost of a partition whose blocks predict their mass-weighted label means. Combining blocks with the same mean implements the same calibrated predictor and preserves cost. Section 4, “An FPTAS?”, still explicitly leaves the fully polynomial approximation question open. [Version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18391), [definition and Fact 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18391v2#S2), [open question](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18391v2#S4).

Qiao's Theorem 1 computes the exact deterministic distance in \(O(|\mathcal X|^4)\) in the real-RAM formulation for uniform masses and noiseless labels, with polynomial overhead for explicit rational inputs. It supplies additive PTAS bounds \(|\mathcal X|^{O(1/\epsilon)}\) under uniform masses and \((|\mathcal X|/\epsilon)^{O(1/\epsilon)}\) in general. Its proof organizes a bounded number of blocks through a multiple-knapsack computation. Thus those guarantees allow the degree to grow with \(1/\epsilon\) and are compatible with the present conditional exclusion. [Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18391v2#S1.SS2).

Qiao's Theorem 2, §§3.3–3.4 and Appendices B–C, already proves exact NP-hardness with noiseless labels and arbitrary rational masses, and with uniform masses and noisy labels. The noiseless hardness reduces from SUBSET SUM and uses a small number of predetermined forecast values. Its exact threshold proof does not supply an inverse-polynomial NO margin on a strongly NP-hard family. The uniform/noisy proof uses a Balanced Subset Sum construction where restricted partition structure has a large separation, but the remaining exact subset-sum equality depends on the encoded perturbations. It does not give the requested full-precision hardness either. The present hard family has nonuniform masses and noiseless labels. It uses a separate anchor forecast for each target bin and starts from strongly NP-complete 3-PARTITION so the normalizing mass is polynomially bounded. [Theorem 2 and proofs](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18391v2#S3).

## Related objectives

- Błasiok, Gopalan, Hu and Nakkiran, *A Unifying Theory of Distance from Calibration*, STOC 2023, introduces the deterministic nearest-calibrated-predictor distance and also studies prediction-only lower and upper variants and smooth surrogates. A surrogate or fractional coupling can use a different feasible set and does not give the exact explicit-atom objective here. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16886).
- Qiao and Zheng, *On the Distance from Calibration in Sequential Prediction*, COLT 2024, §2 and Theorem 2, study deterministic and fractional repairs for unit-weight observations. Later online lower bounds concern rates in sequential prediction. A sum-cost result on separately assignable unit observations does not translate to indivisible nonuniform feature atoms. [COLT paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/qiao24a.html), [Ji and Gu, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16097).
- Wang and Yang, *Sharp Integrality Gaps in Calibration Distance*, arXiv:2610.05679v1, §1 and Theorem 11, provide a rational bit-time approximation to the fractional value and an exactly calibrated repair with an additional additive term equal to the number of source forecast values. Their binary-encoded multiplicities represent separately assignable unit identities. Replacing one weighted atom by identical unit copies would permit splitting that atom and change the feasible set. They explicitly identify this difference. [Definitions and scope](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05679v1#S1), [algorithm](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05679v1#S5).
- The strict integer bounds \(B/4<a_i<B/2\), total \(mB\) and a target of \(m\) bins of load \(B\) are the strongly NP-complete 3-PARTITION problem. In its strongly hard restriction the numeric parameters can be bounded polynomially, which is essential for the runtime contradiction. [Garey and Johnson](https://doi.org/10.1145/322077.322090).

## Release scope and limits

The reduction excludes a deterministic additive FPTAS unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\), including on arbitrary rational masses with deterministic binary labels. If \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\), the rational threshold decision is polynomial by a partition certificate and gives an additive scheme. The same finite-gap reduction would put \(\mathrm{NP}\) in \(\mathrm{BPP}\) if a bounded-error randomized FPTAS existed. Neither the finite replay nor the lower bound classifies uniform noisy instances, randomized or fractional prediction, sample-based estimation, tolerant testing, or a PTAS.

Searches checked Qiao's source and version history, exact-phrase and author/title forward queries, and related work on deterministic repair, unit-weight and weighted atoms, fractional lower distance, online rounding, strong NP-hardness and additive approximation. No earlier inverse-polynomial hardness theorem for Q460's exact deterministic objective appeared in the sources checked. Unindexed or unpublished work and reductions under different terminology remain possible.
